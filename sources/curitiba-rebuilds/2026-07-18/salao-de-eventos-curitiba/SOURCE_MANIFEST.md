## 4. Claims and content that must be omitted or explicitly placeholdered

Do not publish these without additional evidence:

- Exact street address, municipality, map pin, travel time, or claim that the physical venue is inside Curitiba.
- Guest capacity, room dimensions, table layouts, parking capacity, or number of concurrent events.
- Prices, packages, payment terms, menu details, minimums, cancellation terms, availability, response time, or opening hours.
- “Since 2013,” ownership, legal entity/CNPJ, licenses, fire-safety status, accessibility certification, or insurance.
- Awards, rankings, review scores, testimonials, customer counts, event counts, “best,” “most complete,” “safe,” “guaranteed,” or other unverified superiority/performance claims.
- Claims that the category images show this venue or events produced by it.
- Claims that every published service is included in every package or currently available.
- A working contact form, tour, booking calendar, quote calculator, or automated response unless separately implemented, tested, consented to, and disclosed.

## 5. Audience needs (explicit inferences, not demographic facts)

The official occasion categories support three audience hypotheses: people planning weddings; families/guardians and young people planning 15-years celebrations; and graduates or organizing groups planning graduation celebrations. Do not invent age, income, neighborhood, budget, or lifestyle segments.

Shared decision needs inferred from an event-venue journey:

1. Quickly identify whether the venue supports the intended occasion.
2. Understand the physical/technical structure without parsing a long paragraph.
3. Distinguish venue features from services and clarify what is included.
4. See credible, captioned images of the actual space rather than category-only illustrations.
5. Confirm capacity, location, accessibility, policies, availability, and price—currently evidence gaps that should be surfaced as questions or omitted.
6. Reach a verified human contact through a clear, non-deceptive next step.

## 6. Content brief — `index.html`

Purpose: an owner-reviewable public-homepage candidate that combines emotional brand recognition with practical event-planning information. It must read as the venue's site only after approval; until then it remains staging-only.

Core message: begin with the verified promise of being a guest at your own party, then help visitors choose an occasion, understand published infrastructure/services, and reach a verified contact.

Recommended structure:

1. **Header** — text identity or approved logo; links to Ocasiões, Estrutura, Serviços, Galeria, and Contato.
2. **Hero** — first-party promise plus one plain supporting sentence. Prefer a rights-cleared, venue-oriented image (S6) over a generic category image. Primary CTA remains inert/pending until contact confirmation.
3. **Occasion chooser** — Casamento, 15 Anos, Formatura. Keep descriptions neutral; do not invent package details.
4. **Structure** — group verified infrastructure into `Tecnologia`, `Conforto e apoio`, and `Acesso`; preserve precise wording around adapted restrooms and the VIP room.
5. **Services** — list only S1 headings and add `[CONFIRM INCLUDED VS OPTIONAL]` in staging copy or omit package implications.
6. **Support pathway** — paraphrase “da ideia até a conclusão” without inventing process stages, timelines, assigned staff, or guarantees.
7. **Gallery** — use only approved first-party/owner-cleared media with factual captions. Category illustrations may label occasions but must not pose as venue proof.
8. **Contact** — show confirmed phone/WhatsApp and social links; no address/map until confirmed. No live form in this concept.
9. **Footer** — business name, confirmed channels, and normal legal/privacy links only when supplied.

Copy direction: warm, direct, and concrete Portuguese; short headings; avoid inflated luxury language. Keep the existing “convidado” promise as the emotional anchor and use technical facts as practical proof.

Visual direction (design inference):

- Hybrid balance: emotional occasion imagery and an editorial headline system for brand; structured lists, clear labels, and one contact path for usability.
- Use the official logo only with approval. Treat `#1E2A3A`, `#C8A56A`, and `#F7F3ED` as a working palette inferred in `BRAND_SOURCE.md`, not as published brand standards. Validate contrast mechanically.
- Pair a restrained display face for occasion language with a highly legible sans for planning facts; this is a concept choice, not verified existing typography.
- Favor S6 or other owner-cleared gallery images for venue proof. S3–S5 are category illustrations and should be secondary.
- Avoid stock-photo claims, gold-equals-luxury clichés, simulated testimonials, generic card grids, and uncited proof.

Current candidate note: `index.html` already keeps contact masked/inert and states that the address is not established. Before release, replace the custom `SA` mark with the approved logo or a neutral text wordmark; the invented mark is not source-backed.

## 7. Content brief — `proposal.html`

Purpose: a separate, independent sales/design document explaining the redesign opportunity without implying affiliation, publication approval, or guaranteed results.

Required framing: `Conceito independente e não afiliado, preparado apenas para avaliação. Nenhuma alteração foi publicada.` Place once near the top and retain a concise staging label.

Recommended structure:

1. **Opportunity/result** — organize existing first-party brand promise, occasions, structure, and contact path into a clearer decision journey.
2. **Current-state evidence** — page title still contains “Só mais um site WordPress”; the HTTPS homepage references some HTTP media; unrelated template pages remain publicly reachable. Cite S1 and S7.
3. **Three priorities** — (a) occasion-first entry, (b) scannable structure/services and actual-space proof, (c) one verified contact path.
4. **Concept direction** — emotional promise + occasion chooser + practical evidence + contact; distinguish design inference from verified facts.
5. **Deliverables** — responsive homepage candidate, content/evidence manifest, media-rights checklist, and implementation recommendations. Do not imply production deployment.
6. **Dependencies/gaps** — show the eight confirmation placeholders from Section 4.
7. **Next step** — invite internal review/approval of facts, media rights, and channels; do not include outreach automation, a live form, or an owner signature.

Proposal claims to exclude: conversion uplift, recovered revenue, booking growth, SEO ranking, performance gains, awards, competitor comparisons, deadlines, price, or implementation status unless separately measured and authorized.

Current candidate note: `proposal.html` is too brief to satisfy the independent framing and evidence/dependency requirements. Its factual infrastructure list is source-bounded, but it needs the non-affiliation disclosure, cited current-state evidence, explicit dependencies, and a review-only next step before it can be shown externally.
